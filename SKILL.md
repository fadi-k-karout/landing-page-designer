# SKILL.md

## Skill: High-Converting Landing Page Design

### Purpose

Design landing pages that maximize conversions by guiding visitors through a single focused journey from attention to action.

A landing page is NOT a website.

A website offers many paths.

A landing page has ONE goal and ONE primary call-to-action (CTA).

---

# Core Principles

## 1. One Page = One Goal

Every landing page must answer:

- What is being offered?
- Who is it for?
- What action should the visitor take?

Remove competing actions.

Avoid:

- Multiple primary CTAs
- Complex navigation
- Unrelated content
- Multiple offers

Success metric:

A visitor should know what to do within 5 seconds.

---

## 2. Sell Outcomes, Not Features

Bad:

- AI-powered analytics
- Advanced dashboard
- Real-time synchronization

Good:

- Save 5 hours every week
- Spot problems before customers complain
- Keep your team aligned automatically

Use the formula:

Feature → Benefit → Outcome

Example:

Real-time collaboration
→ Everyone sees updates instantly
→ Projects move faster with fewer mistakes

Always answer:

"So what?"

# Required Landing Page Structure

## Section 1: Hero Section

Purpose:
Capture attention immediately.

Required elements:

### Headline

Must communicate:

- Big promise
- Desired outcome
- Transformation

Formula:

[Achieve Result]
without
[Common Pain]

Examples:

- Build Production AI Apps Without Managing Infrastructure
- Launch Your SaaS Landing Page in One Day Without Hiring a Designer

### Supporting Subheadline

Expand the promise.

Include:

- Audience
- Outcome
- Timeframe (if applicable)

### Hero Visual

Use one:

- Product screenshot
- Product mockup
- Demo preview
- Outcome visualization

Avoid decorative imagery.

### Primary CTA

Only one primary action.

Good:

- Start Free Trial
- Get Early Access
- Book Demo

Bad:

- Learn More
- Explore
- Discover

---

## Section 2: Problem & Solution

Purpose:
Make visitors feel understood.

Structure:

### Pain Point

Describe:

- Current frustration
- Cost of inaction
- Existing alternatives failing

### Solution

Position product/service as the bridge.

Use:

Problem
→ Solution
→ Desired Future State

---

## Section 3: Benefits

Purpose:
Show why the solution matters.

Recommended layout:

3 benefit cards

Each card includes:

- Icon
- Benefit headline
- Short explanation

Focus on outcomes.

Not:

❌ Unlimited projects

Instead:

✅ Manage every client from one place

---

## Section 4: Product Demo / Showcase

Purpose:
Prove the product works.

Show:

- Screenshots
- Product walkthroughs
- GIFs
- Before/after examples
- Workflow demonstrations

Visitors should think:

"I can see exactly how this helps me."

Avoid generic stock imagery.

---

## Section 5: Social Proof

Purpose:
Reduce perceived risk.

Priority order:

### Tier 1

- Customer testimonials
- Customer success stories
- Case studies

### Tier 2

- User counts
- Revenue generated
- Customers served

### Tier 3

- Media mentions
- Community mentions
- Social media endorsements

Testimonial formula:

Problem
→ Experience
→ Result

Example:

"We spent 8 hours weekly writing reports. Now it's done in 15 minutes."

---

## Section 6: Offer & Objection Handling

Purpose:
Answer concerns before they arise.

Common objections:

- Too expensive
- Too complicated
- Doesn't fit my use case
- Not enough time
- Not sure it works

Address each directly.

Include:

- FAQs
- Guarantee
- Risk reversal
- Comparison with alternatives

---

## Section 7: Final CTA

Purpose:
Convert visitors who need more information before acting.

Repeat:

- Core value proposition
- Main benefit
- CTA

Keep it simple.

Structure:

Headline
→ Reminder of outcome
→ CTA

# Copywriting Rules

## Clarity Beats Cleverness

Prefer:

"Generate Reports in Seconds"

Over:

"Unlock the Future of Business Intelligence"

---

## Write for Scanners

Use:

- Short paragraphs
- Bullet points
- Strong headings
- Visual hierarchy

---

## Focus on Transformation

Talk about:

Who the customer becomes.

Not:

What the software does.

---

## Maintain Message Consistency

Headline promise must align with:

- Benefits
- Testimonials
- CTA

No conflicting messages.

---

# Visual Design Rules

## Above-the-Fold Matters Most

The first screen must contain:

- Headline
- Subheadline
- CTA
- Visual

No scrolling required.

---

## Use Visual Hierarchy

Order:

1. Headline
2. CTA
3. Visual
4. Supporting content

---

## Minimize Cognitive Load

Remove:

- Excess animations
- Excess text
- Competing colors
- Unnecessary UI elements

Every element must support conversion.

---

# Landing Page Review Checklist

Before approving a landing page:

## Goal

- Is there exactly one primary objective?

## Hero

- Is the value proposition clear within 5 seconds?
- Is the CTA obvious?

## Benefits

- Are benefits outcome-focused?
- Are features translated into value?

## Proof

- Is trust established early?
- Is there meaningful social proof?

## Demonstration

- Can visitors see how the product works?

## Objections

- Are major concerns addressed?

## CTA

- Is the final action clear?

## Simplicity

- Can anything be removed without hurting conversions?

If yes, remove it.

---

# Success Criteria

A successful landing page should make visitors answer "yes" to:

1. Do I understand what this is?
2. Is it for me?
3. Do I believe it works?
4. Do I want the outcome?
5. Do I know what to do next?

If any answer is "no", improve the page before launch.
